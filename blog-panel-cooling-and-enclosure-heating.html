Panel Cooling & Enclosure Heating

Maintenance · Published 2026-07-12 · Apt Controls Team

Two seasons, two failure modes. In summer, drives trip on over-temperature at 3 pm; in monsoon, condensation grows corrosion on PCBs overnight. Both trace back to the same neglected subject: the climate inside the control panel.

Heat: the summer killer

Every VFD, power supply and transformer inside a panel dissipates heat — typically 3–5% of the power it handles. In a sealed panel under an Indian summer roof, internal temperatures easily run 15–20 °C above ambient. Electronics age fast in that heat: as a rule of thumb, every 10 °C above rated temperature roughly halves electronic component life. The failure isn't dramatic — it's the drive that trips every afternoon, the power supply that dies at 3 years instead of 10.

Fixing it

  • Filter fan units — the standard fix: a fan pushing filtered ambient air through the panel, with an exhaust filter diagonally opposite for a proper airflow path. Size by the panel's heat load (sum the dissipation of drives, PSUs and transformers) and the allowed temperature rise.
  • Placement: intake low and cool, exhaust high — heat rises. Never let a fan pull hot air straight from one drive into another.
  • Maintenance: a clogged filter mat turns a cooled panel back into a sealed one. Put filter mats on the same schedule as motor greasing.
  • Beyond fans: where ambient air itself is too hot or too dirty, step up to heat exchangers or panel air conditioners — but most panels in most plants are solved by correctly sized fan-and-filter pairs.

Condensation: the monsoon killer

When a panel cools overnight — or sits in an air-conditioned room with humid outside air leaking in — moisture condenses on the coldest metal inside. Over weeks, that means corroded terminals, tracking across PCBs, and mystery earth faults that vanish by mid-morning. Outdoor kiosks, weighbridge panels and coastal or riverside sites suffer most.

Fixing it

  • Anti-condensation heaters — a small enclosure heater (often just 10–100 W) keeps the internal air a few degrees above dew point so moisture never condenses. Control it with a thermostat or, better, a hygrostat that switches on humidity.
  • Seal integrity: heaters can't outrun an open door or a missing cable-gland plug. Maintain gaskets and glands — IP54 on the nameplate means nothing after five ungrommeted drillings.
  • Drainage: for outdoor enclosures, fit breather/drain plugs so any moisture that does enter can leave.

A 10-minute panel climate audit

  • Measure internal temperature on a hot afternoon — is it within the ratings of the hottest-running device?
  • Check filter mats: when were they last changed?
  • Look for rust spots, green corrosion or water stains — evidence of condensation
  • Outdoor/humid-area panels: is there a heater, and does it actually switch?
  • Count open gland holes and missing plugs
