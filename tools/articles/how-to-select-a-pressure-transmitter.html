A pressure transmitter looks like a simple purchase — until the wrong one arrives. Wrong range, wrong thread, wrong wetted parts, and your commissioning date slips while a replacement ships. Here is the checklist we run through with every enquiry.

1. Fix the measuring range correctly

Choose a range so that your normal operating pressure sits between 30% and 70% of full scale. A 0–10 bar line running at 6 bar is well served by a 0–10 bar transmitter; putting a 0–100 bar unit on it wastes accuracy, while a 0–6 bar unit lives at full scale and dies early. Always account for pressure spikes — water hammer in pump lines can momentarily exceed the static pressure several times over, so check the transmitter's overpressure rating, not just its range.

2. Gauge, absolute or differential?

  • Gauge pressure (relative to atmosphere) covers most industrial applications — pumps, compressors, process lines.
  • Absolute pressure is needed for vacuum processes, distillation and altitude-independent measurement.
  • Differential pressure measures across filters, orifice plates and heat exchangers — and calculates tank level from hydrostatic head.

3. Accuracy — pay only for what the loop needs

Standard industrial transmitters offer 0.5% or 0.25% of span, which satisfies most monitoring and control duties. Custody transfer, test benches and pharma-critical loops justify 0.1% or better. Remember that total loop accuracy includes the indicator or PLC analog input — a 0.075% transmitter feeding a 0.5% input card gains you nothing.

4. Process connection and wetted parts

Confirm the thread standard before ordering — ½" BSP and ½" NPT look similar and seal differently. For corrosive media, check chemical compatibility of the diaphragm and seals: SS316L handles most utilities, but chlorides, acids and slurries may need Hastelloy, tantalum or a diaphragm seal with PTFE lining. Hygienic processes need tri-clamp connections and flush diaphragms.

5. Output signal and wiring

The 2-wire 4–20 mA loop remains the industrial default — simple, noise-immune and failure-indicating (a broken loop reads 0 mA, clearly a fault). Choose HART if your plant uses smart configuration or asset management. On modern machines, IO-Link transmitters add remote configuration and diagnostics over standard sensor cable.

6. Environment

Check ingress protection (IP65 minimum outdoors, IP67/IP69K for washdown), ambient temperature at the mounting point, and hazardous-area certification where flammable atmospheres exist. High process temperatures may need a cooling element or impulse-line mounting rather than direct insertion.

The 60-second checklist

  • Range: normal pressure at 30–70% of span; spikes within overpressure rating
  • Type: gauge / absolute / differential
  • Accuracy: matched to the loop, not the brochure
  • Connection: thread standard confirmed; wetted parts compatible with media
  • Output: 4–20 mA (HART/IO-Link where useful)
  • Environment: IP rating, temperature, hazardous area
